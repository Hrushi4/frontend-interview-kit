Context

InterpretIQ lets patients and healthcare providers talk through a medical interpreter on a live video call. If a call drops, a consultation stops until everyone is back, so reliability matters more than features.

The app is access-gated, runs as a PWA, and must meet WCAG accessibility requirements.

Constraints

  • Networks. Calls run on hospital and clinic networks, which can be slow, change mid-call, or block UDP traffic.
  • Accessibility. The app has to be WCAG-compliant. Call controls must work with a keyboard and screen readers.
  • Privacy. Access is gated, and patient health information must be protected in storage, logs and tokens.
  • Scale. Up to 150 concurrent sessions.

Key decisions

1. Keep the video room out of React state

Options
Store the LiveKit room in Redux; keep it in a component ref; or wrap it in a small call service and store only plain call state in Redux.
Choice
A call service owns the room. Redux holds plain, serialisable state (status, participants, mute) for the UI.
Trade-off
Two places to keep in sync, so every room event goes through one handler that updates Redux.

2. Resume first, then rejoin

Options
Rely on the SDK defaults; always do a full rejoin; or try to resume the session and fall back to a full rejoin with a fresh token.
Choice
Resume first and rejoin second, and allow a TURN relay over TLS on port 443 for networks that block UDP.
Trade-off
More states to design and test (reconnecting, relayed, failed), and relayed calls cost more bandwidth.

3. Self-host LiveKit on AWS

Options
A managed video service, or self-hosted LiveKit on AWS.
Choice
Designed a self-hosted setup with Docker, CI/CD, caching, a CDN and monitoring. It is projected to cut video costs by 40% and handle 10× the traffic.
Trade-off
The team takes on operations: scaling, upgrades, certificates and on-call.
